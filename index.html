What this is

A worksheet that estimates how much money a seller actually walks away with, at up to five different sale prices, for a house in unincorporated King County or the city of Renton.

Most online calculators give you one number and hide the assumptions. This one shows every deduction as its own line so you can argue with any of them, and it separates the money you receive at the closing table from the money you keep after taxes. Those are two different numbers that arrive at two different times, and treating them as one is the most common way sellers get surprised.

How to use it

  1. Pick how many scenarios you want. One is enough to answer "what do I walk away with." Use two or more when you're weighing a price cut and want to see what it really costs you.
  2. Enter the price, then nudge the slider. Dragging shows you how proceeds move continuously, which is more useful than guessing at three fixed numbers.
  3. Fill in the costs of sale. Commission percentages come from your listing agreement. Fixed closing costs come from an escrow quote or a net sheet.
  4. Set the closing date. It drives the property tax proration and the capital gains holding period. Both change with the calendar.
  5. Fill in the capital gain section. For a recently purchased home this can be the largest number on the page.
  6. Read the two bottom lines. The green figure is cash at closing. The dark figure is what you keep after federal tax. The bar pinned to the bottom of the screen tracks both; tap it to switch.

Everything recalculates as you type, and your entries are saved automatically. Tap any line item on a results card to see what it means without leaving the page.

Solve for a target

If you know what you need to walk away with, open the "Solve for a target" panel, enter that number, and the app works backwards to the sale price that produces it. Then apply the answer to any scenario. This is usually the more honest way to approach a listing decision than picking a price and hoping.

Wait or sell now

If you're under the two-year capital gains mark, the "Wait or sell now" panel compares the tax you'd save by holding until you qualify against what holding actually costs: mortgage payment, property tax, insurance, and any expected price movement in the meantime. It gives you a dollar answer rather than a feeling.

Costs of sale

FieldWhat to enter
Listing agentThe percentage your own agent's brokerage charges. From your listing agreement. Negotiable.
Buyer agentWhat you're offering to the buyer's agent. Since 2024 this is no longer advertised through the MLS and is negotiated deal by deal, but most sellers still pay something. Often the first thing to move in a slow market.

FieldWhat to enter
Closing dateThe day the sale records. Drives both the tax proration and the capital gains holding period.
Annual property taxYour full-year bill. Look up the parcel on the King County Assessor's eReal Property tool, or read it off your most recent statement.
Paid so far this yearWhether you've paid nothing, the first half, or the full year. If a lender pays your taxes from escrow, check with them rather than assuming.

You owe property tax for the days you owned the house. Prepaid past your closing date and the buyer reimburses you, shown as a green credit. Behind, and you're debited at settlement. It is almost never zero, which is why a net sheet showing $0.00 here is a placeholder rather than a calculation.

Federal capital gain

FieldWhat to enter
Purchase dateWhen you bought. Determines whether you qualify for the exclusion, so get it exactly right.
Purchase priceWhat you paid, from your closing statement.
Buy-side costsCosts paid when buying that add to basis: title, escrow, recording, transfer taxes, survey, legal. Not loan points, prepaid interest, or prepaid property tax.

How the excise tax works

Washington charges the seller a real estate excise tax, or REET, on the full sale price. Not the profit, not the equity, not the amount above the mortgage. Sellers who break even still owe it in full.

It's tiered, so different slices of the price are taxed at different rates. Only the portion inside each band is taxed at that band's rate. The rates below combine the state's graduated schedule with the 0.50% local rate that applies in both unincorporated King County and the city of Renton.

Portion of sale priceCombined rate
Up to $525,0001.60% (1.10% state + 0.50% local)
$525,000.01 to $1,525,0001.78% (1.28% state + 0.50% local)
$1,525,000.01 to $3,025,0003.25% (2.75% state + 0.50% local)
Above $3,025,0003.50% (3.00% state + 0.50% local)

Worked example at a $925,000 sale price:

First $525,000 × 1.60% = $8,400.00
Next $400,000 × 1.78% = $7,120.00
 
Total excise tax = $15,520.00
Effective rate = 1.678%

Because the tiers are marginal rather than all-or-nothing, pricing just under a threshold saves less than most people expect. The saving is only the rate difference on the amount above the line, not a rate change on the whole sale.

Verify before closing. The Department of Revenue readjusts these price thresholds periodically, and the current values took effect January 1, 2023. Local rates are republished on their own schedule. If your closing moves into a new year, pull the current rate sheet from the Department of Revenue rather than trusting this page.

The two-year rule, in plain English

Section 121 of the tax code lets you exclude up to $250,000 of gain from federal tax if you're single, or $500,000 if married filing jointly. It is the largest tax break most homeowners will ever get.

To qualify you must have owned the home and lived in it as your main home for at least 2 years out of the 5 years before the sale. The two years don't have to be consecutive.

  • It's a cliff, not a slope. At 729 days you get nothing and the entire gain is taxable. At 730 days you get the full exclusion. Nothing else in this worksheet behaves that way, and no commission negotiation is worth as much as clearing that date.
  • Under one year is worse still. Owning 12 months or less makes the gain short-term, taxed as ordinary income at your regular bracket rather than at capital gains rates.
  • There are exceptions. Selling early for a job relocation, health problem, or certain unforeseen circumstances can earn a prorated portion. The rules are specific. Ask a CPA rather than assuming you qualify.

Washington itself does not tax this. The state's capital gains excise tax specifically excludes all real estate, primary residence and investment property alike. Only the federal tax applies.

What this does not include

  • HOA dues, transfer fees, and resale certificate charges
  • Per-diem interest and reconveyance fees inside the loan payoff
  • Repair credits beyond what you enter as concessions
  • Home warranty, pest inspection, and other seller-elective items
  • Junior liens, judgments, second mortgages, HELOC balances
  • Moving, storage, staging, and pre-listing repairs
  • Depreciation recapture, which applies if the home was ever a rental
  • Bracket effects: a large short-term gain can push you into a higher bracket than the flat rate you entered
